# Methodology Notes

The repository uses synthetic data to demonstrate behavioral-economics concepts. Models are deliberately simplified so assumptions remain visible.

Common modeling layers include:

1. Classical expected-utility benchmarks.
2. Behavioral modifiers such as framing, loss aversion, heuristic sensitivity, present bias, effort cost, social influence, and trust.
3. Aggregate outcomes such as uptake, cooperation, savings behavior, platform retention, or policy participation.
4. Documentation of assumptions, limitations, and ethical considerations.

Real empirical research requires research design, measurement validity, identification strategy, external validity checks, and domain-specific ethical review.
