# Digital Finance Design Notes

Digital-finance interfaces shape mental accounting by deciding which balances, labels, payments, debts, subscriptions, and future obligations are visible.

## User-aligned designs

- Show category-level budgets and total household exposure together.
- Present high-interest debt and liquid savings in the same view.
- Offer windfall-splitting prompts for spending, saving, and debt repayment.
- Preserve labeled savings while reminding users of opportunity cost.
- Make subscriptions, fees, and buy-now-pay-later obligations visible.
- Allow users to rename and revise account labels.
- Avoid hiding repayment obligations behind convenience.

## Risky or exploitative designs

- Store credits that encourage additional spending.
- Rewards that make spending feel like earning.
- Subscription systems that exploit inattention.
- Buy-now-pay-later interfaces that separate consumption from repayment.
- Apps that show "available to spend" while hiding upcoming obligations.
- Rigid account restrictions that harm households facing emergencies.

The goal is not to eliminate mental accounting. It is to design financial interfaces that preserve useful categories while making cross-account tradeoffs visible.
