# Financial-Stability Notes

Herd behavior becomes systemically important when imitation interacts with leverage, liquidity fragility, common risk models, benchmark constraints, redemption pressure, and crowded positioning.

A stylized systemic-risk expression is:

```text
Systemic Herding Risk = Crowded Exposure x Leverage Pressure / Liquidity Depth
```

## Key diagnostics

- Buy-rate synchronization.
- Price deviation from baseline or fundamental proxy.
- Boom-bust range.
- Maximum drawdown after shock.
- Volatility proxy.
- Liquidity-adjusted price impact.
- Crowd-signal sensitivity.
- Social-media amplification.
- Reputation-pressure intensity.
- Strategy concentration.

## Governance relevance

Supervisors and risk managers should distinguish:

- informative social learning from self-referential imitation
- common information from true herding
- price discovery from price-chasing
- liquidity from crowded-exit fragility
- retail participation from manipulative promotion
- conventional institutional risk management from systemic synchronization
