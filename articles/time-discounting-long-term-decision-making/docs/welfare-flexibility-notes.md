# Welfare and Flexibility Notes

Time-discounting interventions should be evaluated by welfare, not delayed choice alone. A lower discount rate, stronger commitment device, or more future-oriented default may increase long-term action while reducing flexibility for people facing immediate hardship.

A stylized welfare expression is:

```text
Welfare = Future Gains + Goal Progress + Liquidity/Flexibility Value - Immediate Costs - Behavioral Burden - Hardship Cost
```

## Core welfare questions

- Does the intervention help people act on future-oriented goals they endorse?
- Does it preserve flexibility during genuine hardship?
- Does it reduce future harm without worsening present insecurity?
- Does it address structural constraints or merely shift responsibility to individuals?
- Does the institution benefit from the user's present-oriented behavior?
- Are intergenerational benefits and present distributional burdens evaluated together?

## Recommended diagnostics

- Delayed-choice rate by regime.
- Cumulative delayed choices by regime.
- Cumulative welfare by regime.
- Treatment effects by present-bias quartile.
- Treatment effects by discount-factor quartile.
- Treatment effects by liquidity-need quartile.
- Sensitivity to discount rates, commitment support, flexibility, and future-goal values.
- Welfare comparison of present-biased and commitment-supported regimes.
