7. Estimate heterogeneity by wealth quartile.
8. Estimate heterogeneity by income-security quartile.
9. Estimate heterogeneity by numeracy quartile.
10. Estimate heterogeneity by prior-loss exposure.
11. Estimate heterogeneity by trust quartile.
12. Vary reference points.
13. Vary gain/loss ratios.
14. Vary probability-weighting parameters.
15. Vary loss-aversion parameters.
16. Compare low-probability gain, low-probability loss, high-probability gain, and high-probability loss domains.
17. Compare behavior-change metrics with welfare metrics.
18. Test distributional sensitivity in policy-transition examples.

## External validity

Real-world prospect-theory effects depend on reference points, income security, wealth, trust, institutional history, cognitive load, numeracy, policy design, platform interfaces, lived exposure to risk, and structural constraints.
