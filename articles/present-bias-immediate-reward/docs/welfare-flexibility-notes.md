# Welfare and Flexibility Notes

Present-bias interventions should be evaluated by welfare, not behavior change alone. A stronger commitment device may increase delayed choice while reducing flexibility for people facing emergency needs, unstable income, health constraints, or caregiving obligations.

A stylized welfare expression is:

```text
Welfare = Future Gains + Goal Progress + Flexibility Value - Immediate Costs - Behavioral Burden - Hardship Cost
```

## Core welfare questions

- Does the intervention help people act on reflective long-term goals?
- Does it preserve flexibility during genuine hardship?
- Does it reduce future harm without increasing present distress?
- Does it protect autonomy and meaningful exit?
- Does it address structural constraints or merely shift responsibility to individuals?
- Does the institution benefit from the user’s present bias?
- Are outcomes evaluated across heterogeneous groups?

## Recommended diagnostics

- Delayed-choice rate by regime.
- Cumulative delayed choices by regime.
- Cumulative welfare by regime.
- Treatment effects by present-bias quartile.
- Treatment effects by liquidity-need quartile.
- Treatment effects by sophistication quartile.
- Sensitivity to commitment cost, reminder strength, and flexibility.
- Welfare comparison of medium versus strong commitment.
