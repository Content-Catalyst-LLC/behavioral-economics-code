# Welfare and Flexibility Notes

Commitment devices should be evaluated by welfare, not compliance alone. A stronger commitment may increase target behavior while imposing hardship when people face emergencies, liquidity shocks, or changing circumstances.

A stylized welfare expression is:

```text
Welfare = LongTermBenefit + FlexibilityValue - ImmediateCost - BehavioralBurden - HardshipCost
```

## Core welfare questions

- Does the commitment device improve long-term welfare or only increase measured compliance?
- Does it protect liquidity during emergencies?
- Does it help strongly present-biased agents more than calibrated agents?
- Does it preserve meaningful exit?
- Does it punish people facing income volatility?
- Is commitment voluntary, defaulted, or imposed?
- Does the institution benefit from inertia or penalties?
- Is the user’s reflective goal plausibly served?

## Recommended diagnostics

- Accumulated savings by regime.
- Welfare by regime.
- Emergency withdrawal by regime.
- Hardship cost by regime.
- Treatment effects by present-bias quartile.
- Treatment effects by liquidity-need quartile.
- Treatment effects by sophistication quartile.
- Sensitivity to commitment cost, automation strength, and flexibility.
