# Investor-Protection Notes

Behavioral finance is most useful for policy when it connects investor autonomy with decision environments that reduce avoidable harm.

## Key diagnostics

- Trading frequency and turnover relative to investor goals.
- Cost drag as a share of gross returns.
- Diversification and concentration.
- Product complexity and leverage access.
- Salience of short-term price movement.
- Friction around high-risk trades.
- Disclosure comprehension, not merely disclosure availability.
- Reference-point and loss-realization behavior.
- Herd-signal exposure through social feeds or trending assets.
- Mispricing, volatility, and drawdown during high-attention regimes.

## Governance relevance

Investor-protection analysis should distinguish:

- access from manipulation
- education from disclosure overload
- engagement from welfare
- social learning from herding
- investor choice from exploitative decision architecture
- legitimate active management from costly overtrading
