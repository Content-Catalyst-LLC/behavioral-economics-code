# Benchmark Design Notes

Anchors are unavoidable in public policy, consumer markets, labor markets, housing, sustainability, and platform design. The relevant question is whether they are accurate, transparent, and welfare-supporting.

## Common economic anchors

- List prices.
- Suggested retail prices.
- Opening offers.
- Salary ranges.
- Prior salary.
- Appraisal values.
- Purchase prices.
- Analyst targets.
- 52-week highs.
- Monthly payments.
- Credit limits.
- Default contribution rates.
- Budget baselines.
- Regulatory thresholds.
- Poverty thresholds.
- Carbon baselines.
- Historical weather or emissions baselines.
- Algorithmic estimates.

## Benchmark review questions

- Who set the anchor?
- Is it evidence-based?
- Does it reflect current conditions?
- Does it embed historical injustice or outdated assumptions?
- Does it help the decision-maker or the institution setting it?
- Is uncertainty disclosed?
- Can affected people contest it?
- Are distributional consequences measured?
- Is the benchmark periodically updated?

Anchors should be treated as design choices with economic and ethical consequences.
