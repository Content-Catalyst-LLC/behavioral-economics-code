# Sustainability Baseline Notes

Anchoring matters for sustainability because baselines shape perceptions of ecological change, climate ambition, infrastructure adequacy, and transition cost.

## Sustainability anchors

- Historical emissions baselines.
- Prior energy prices.
- Past weather patterns.
- Historical flood maps.
- Old infrastructure standards.
- Legacy utility planning assumptions.
- Prior biodiversity levels.
- Recent ecological memory.
- Carbon budgets.
- Net-zero dates.
- Adaptation-cost estimates.
- Regulatory thresholds.

## Risks

- Outdated baselines can normalize degradation.
- Short memory can produce shifting-baseline effects.
- Historical infrastructure anchors can understate future climate risk.
- Emissions targets can look ambitious or weak depending on baseline year.
- Energy-cost anchors can delay clean-energy investment when price assumptions are stale.

## Better practices

- Use science-informed baselines.
- Disclose baseline years and assumptions.
- Include long-horizon historical context.
- Update benchmarks as evidence changes.
- Compare multiple baselines.
- Include justice and vulnerability analysis.
- Avoid using convenient baselines to minimize responsibility.
