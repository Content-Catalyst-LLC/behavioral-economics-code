# Labor and Wage Bargaining Notes

Anchors matter in labor markets because compensation negotiations often begin from posted salary ranges, prior salary, internal bands, employer opening offers, or candidate expectations.

## Anchoring risks

- Low posted ranges can suppress bargaining.
- Prior salary can reproduce historical pay inequity.
- Wide salary bands can provide false transparency.
- Initial employer offers can define the negotiation range.
- Workers with less bargaining power may adjust more from employer anchors.
- Algorithmic compensation tools may obscure the benchmark logic.

## Better practices

- Use clear salary ranges.
- Avoid asking for prior salary where it reproduces inequity.
- Explain compensation criteria.
- Audit ranges for gender, race, disability, and class effects.
- Track outcomes by bargaining power and demographic vulnerability.
- Treat salary bands as public-interest labor-market infrastructure, not merely HR convenience.
