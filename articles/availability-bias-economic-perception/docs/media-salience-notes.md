# Media Salience Notes

Media systems influence availability bias by shaping which events become repeatedly visible, emotionally memorable, and socially discussable.

## Salience channels

- Repeated headlines.
- Crisis imagery.
- Viral clips.
- Personal stories.
- Conflict framing.
- Outrage cues.
- Market-crash charts.
- Disaster footage.
- Celebrity or elite examples.
- Social repetition.
- Algorithmic recommendation.
- Push notifications.
- Trending-topic labels.

## Analytical cautions

- Coverage intensity is not equivalent to statistical frequency.
- A vivid story can still reveal a real structural problem.
- Slow-moving risks may be undercovered because they lack event form.
- Rare harms affecting powerful groups may receive more coverage than common harms affecting marginalized groups.
- Public risk perception may reflect media availability rather than objective incidence.

## Responsible communication

- Pair vivid examples with base rates.
- Use denominators and historical comparisons.
- Include uncertainty ranges where appropriate.
- Avoid fear amplification without agency.
- Make slow structural risks visible.
- Protect communities from being reduced to illustrative disaster cases.
