# Availability Bias and Economic Perception

This article-level folder supports the article **Availability Bias and Economic Perception**.

Article URL: https://sustainablecatalyst.com/availability-bias-economic-perception/  
Repository folder: https://github.com/Content-Catalyst-LLC/behavioral-economics-code/tree/main/articles/availability-bias-economic-perception

## Purpose

This is a professional economist-facing computational scaffold for studying availability bias, the availability heuristic, economic perception, perceived probability, risk calibration, media salience, vividness, recency, social repetition, base-rate correction, investor sentiment, insurance demand, public policy support, crisis communication, digital attention systems, sustainability risk, climate communication, and institutional risk governance.

The scaffold is designed for behavioral economics, applied microeconomics, household finance, behavioral finance, insurance economics, public economics, risk regulation, digital-platform governance, media economics, climate economics, sustainability policy, health economics, institutional design, and reproducible teaching examples.

## Research questions

1. How do salience, recency, vividness, media exposure, and social repetition distort perceived probability?
2. How does base-rate disclosure reduce calibration error?
3. How do availability-sensitive agents respond to high-salience risk environments?
4. How do numeracy and trust in statistics moderate availability bias?
5. When does risk communication improve calibration, and when does it merely intensify fear?
6. How do availability effects alter risky-asset participation, insurance demand, and policy support?
7. How do digital platforms amplify or correct availability bias through attention architecture?
8. How does availability bias shape climate-risk perception, disaster preparedness, and sustainability governance?
9. What robustness checks are required before treating a salience intervention as welfare-improving?

## Econometric and computational design

The scaffold includes synthetic availability-regime data that support:

- salience and recall simulations
- perceived-probability distortion models
- objective versus subjective probability comparisons
- base-rate correction workflows
- calibration-error diagnostics
- investor-sentiment examples
- insurance-demand models
- policy-support simulations
- crisis-communication scaffolds
- media-salience and digital-platform notes
- sustainability and climate-risk communication notes
- treatment-effect estimation
- heterogeneous treatment effects by availability sensitivity, numeracy, trust, risk tolerance, and prior experience
- welfare proxy analysis
- robustness and sensitivity checks
- Stata, R, Python, Julia, SQL, and systems-language examples

## Responsible use

This repository is for synthetic-data research, methods demonstration, policy-evaluation scaffolding, consumer-protection analysis, risk-communication review, climate-risk communication, digital-platform governance, public-interest institutional learning, and reproducible behavioral-economics workflows. It is not a tool for fear-based persuasion, panic amplification, manipulative risk messaging, deceptive marketing, engagement-maximizing sensationalism, or using behavioral insights to bypass informed judgment.
