# Welfare Analysis Notes

Behavioral environmental policy should not be evaluated only by uptake. Adoption is important, but a policy can increase adoption while creating high fiscal cost, unequal access, excessive administrative burden, or weak environmental benefit.

## Welfare components

The synthetic workflows define total welfare as a function of:

- household/private benefit
- environmental benefit
- fiscal cost
- administrative cost
- friction cost
- energy-burden interaction
- distributional incidence

## Welfare accounting

A stylized welfare expression is:

```text
W = private_benefit + environmental_benefit - fiscal_cost - administrative_cost - friction_cost
```

## Distributional welfare

The scaffold reports welfare by:

- income quintile
- energy-burden group
- present-bias quartile
- institutional-trust quartile

## Policy relevance

This scaffold supports:

- environmental program uptake evaluation
- climate policy implementation analysis
- green default policy evaluation
- subsidy and rebate take-up analysis
- energy burden and distributional incidence analysis
- environmental justice-oriented program design
