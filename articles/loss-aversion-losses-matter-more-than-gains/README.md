# Loss Aversion: Why Losses Matter More Than Gains

This article-level folder supports the article **Loss Aversion: Why Losses Matter More Than Gains**.

Article URL: https://sustainablecatalyst.com/loss-aversion-losses-matter-more-than-gains/  
Repository folder: https://github.com/Content-Catalyst-LLC/behavioral-economics-code/tree/main/articles/loss-aversion-losses-matter-more-than-gains

## Purpose

This is a professional economist-facing computational scaffold for studying loss aversion, reference dependence, prospect-theory value functions, gain/loss framing, mixed gambles, disposition effects, endowment effects, consumer loss framing, wage and bargaining reference points, policy-transition loss distributions, sustainability transition resistance, digital-platform loss framing, welfare analysis, and institutional decision design.

The scaffold is designed for behavioral economics, applied microeconomics, behavioral finance, consumer protection, labor economics, housing economics, public economics, platform governance, climate economics, sustainability policy, institutional economics, and reproducible teaching examples.

## Research questions

1. How do gain frames and loss frames change risky choice under reference-dependent valuation?
2. How does the loss-aversion coefficient change acceptance of mixed gambles?
3. How do purchase-price reference points produce the disposition effect?
4. How does ownership or perceived entitlement create endowment-effect behavior?
5. How do consumer fees, subscriptions, and cancellation flows use loss framing?
6. How do wage cuts, benefit losses, and status losses shape labor-market bargaining?
7. How do policy transitions create concentrated losses and diffuse gains?
8. How does sustainability policy become politically difficult when short-term losses are salient and long-term gains are diffuse?
9. How should analysts distinguish real material loss from perceived loss, unjust entitlement, or manipulative framing?
10. What robustness checks are required before interpreting loss-framed interventions as welfare-improving?

## Econometric and computational design

The scaffold includes synthetic loss-aversion and reference-dependent-choice data that support:

- prospect-theory value-function examples
- gain-frame and loss-frame simulations
- mixed-gamble acceptance models
- reference-point sensitivity analysis
- disposition-effect simulations
- endowment-effect examples
- consumer loss-framing workflows
- wage and bargaining reference-point scaffolds
- policy-transition and loss-distribution models
- sustainability-transition simulations
- digital-platform loss-framing notes
- treatment-effect-style estimation by frame
- heterogeneity by loss-aversion intensity, income security, prior loss exposure, numeracy, and trust
- welfare and distributional sensitivity analysis
- robustness checks
- Stata, R, Python, Julia, SQL, and systems-language examples

## Responsible use

This repository is for synthetic-data research, methods demonstration, policy-evaluation scaffolding, consumer-protection analysis, behavioral-finance research, labor-market transition analysis, sustainability governance, platform accountability, institutional learning, and reproducible behavioral-economics workflows. It is not a toolkit for manipulative fear framing, dark-pattern cancellation design, deceptive scarcity messages, exploitative pricing, predatory trading interfaces, anti-worker wage framing, or using behavioral insights to bypass informed judgment.
