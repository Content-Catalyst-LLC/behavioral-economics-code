# Labor, Wages, and Bargaining Notes

Loss aversion matters in labor markets because compensation, benefits, schedules, remote work, status, and autonomy become reference points.

## Key channels

- nominal wage rigidity
- resistance to wage cuts
- benefit loss
- flexibility loss
- seniority loss
- status loss
- job-security loss
- bargaining reference points
- prior salary anchors
- transition costs

## Policy interpretation

Labor-market loss aversion should be treated with care. Workers facing job loss, wage cuts, benefit reductions, displacement, or occupational decline are not merely displaying bias. They may be experiencing real material harm.

Just transition, wage insurance, retraining, worker voice, regional investment, and benefit protection are important when policy creates concentrated labor losses.
