# Welfare Analysis Notes

Fairness and reciprocity should not be evaluated only by observed compliance or cooperation. Higher cooperation is valuable when cooperation is reciprocal and fair. It can reduce welfare when it reflects coerced compliance, weak exit options, or trust extraction.

A stylized welfare expression is:

```text
W = material_payoff + fairness_utility + reciprocity_utility + process_legitimacy - rejection_cost - punishment_cost - enforcement_cost
```

## Core welfare questions

- Does process fairness reduce rejection without hiding exploitation?
- Does reciprocity improve cooperation and welfare when material allocations are unequal?
- Does unfairness increase enforcement costs?
- Are high-fairness-sensitivity groups helped or harmed by institutional design?
- Does a regime improve real fairness or merely fairness perception?

## Recommended diagnostics

- Material payoff ranking versus fairness-reciprocity utility ranking.
- Treatment effects by fairness-sensitivity quartile.
- Treatment effects by reciprocity-sensitivity quartile.
- Welfare sensitivity to rejection costs and punishment costs.
- Process-fairness sensitivity analysis.
- Distinction between voluntary cooperation and constrained compliance.
