# Behavioral Departures Notes

Expected utility is the rational-choice benchmark. Behavioral economics studies systematic departures from that benchmark.

## Common departures

- Loss aversion.
- Reference dependence.
- Probability weighting.
- Certainty effect.
- Allais paradox.
- Ellsberg paradox.
- Ambiguity aversion.
- Availability bias.
- Anchoring.
- Framing effects.
- Present bias.
- Mental accounting.
- Regret aversion.
- Status quo bias.

## Empirical caution

An observed departure from expected utility should not automatically be labeled irrational. It may reflect constraints, trust, local knowledge, ambiguity, vulnerability, institutional history, or moral preference.
