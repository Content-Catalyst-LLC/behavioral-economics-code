# Sustainability Governance Notes

Sustainability decisions are vulnerable to heuristics because they involve long horizons, systemic risk, uncertainty, diffuse causation, and unequal vulnerability.

## Behavioral challenges

- Availability bias makes visible disasters more persuasive than slow structural risk.
- Present bias undervalues long-term prevention.
- Anchoring to recent baselines normalizes ecological degradation.
- Framing affects whether policy feels like sacrifice, security, stewardship, opportunity, or justice.
- Status quo bias protects fossil, infrastructure, and consumption systems.
- Representativeness can turn a few examples into misleading generalizations.

## Responsible design

- Make slow risks visible.
- Use historical and forward-looking baselines.
- Present uncertainty honestly.
- Link stories to evidence.
- Use justice-aware framing.
- Avoid catastrophe-only communication.
- Pair urgency with agency.
- Design defaults that support public welfare.
- Include local knowledge and marginalized experience.
