# Heuristic Taxonomy

## Core heuristics

### Availability

Judgment is shaped by ease of recall. Vivid, recent, repeated, and emotionally salient examples become easier to retrieve and can distort perceived probability.

### Representativeness

Judgment is shaped by resemblance to familiar patterns. This can produce base-rate neglect, small-sample overinterpretation, and overconfident narratives.

### Anchoring

Judgment is shaped by an initial reference point. Estimates often remain too close to the starting value because adjustment is incomplete.

### Framing

Judgment is shaped by how equivalent information is presented. Gain/loss frames, attribute frames, risk frames, and social frames can alter interpretation.

## Related biases

- Hindsight bias.
- Status quo bias.
- Present bias.
- Mental accounting.
- Herd behavior.
- Ambiguity aversion.
- Optimism bias.
- Base-rate neglect.
- Planning fallacy.

## Economist-facing interpretation

Heuristics should not be treated as moral failures or generalized irrationality. They are cognitive strategies whose quality depends on environment, incentives, institutions, feedback, and power.
