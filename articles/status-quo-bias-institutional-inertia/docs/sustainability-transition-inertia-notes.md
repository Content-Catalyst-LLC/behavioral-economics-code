# Sustainability Transition Inertia Notes

Status quo bias matters for sustainability because carbon-intensive systems are embedded in infrastructure, finance, policy, habits, and expectations.

## Status quo channels in sustainability

- Existing fossil-fuel infrastructure.
- Car-dependent land use.
- Industrial agriculture.
- Legacy utility regulation.
- Procurement habits.
- Incumbent industry lobbying.
- Consumer familiarity.
- Short-term cost salience.
- Worker and community transition risk.
- Institutional uncertainty about alternatives.

## Policy responses

- Reduce switching costs.
- Provide transition support.
- Use credible long-term standards.
- Build infrastructure for alternatives.
- Protect workers and communities.
- Make sustainable defaults easy.
- Use phased implementation.
- Compensate concentrated losses where appropriate.
- Track adoption and welfare.
- Avoid imposing present burdens on vulnerable households.

Sustainability transitions require overcoming behavioral inertia and material path dependence while preserving justice.
