# Institutional Inertia and Path Dependence Notes

Institutions preserve status quos through routines, budgets, legal rules, procurement, technology, professional identity, stakeholder coalitions, and political incentives.

## Inertia mechanisms

- Legacy technology.
- Sunk-cost reasoning.
- Organizational risk aversion.
- Budget cycles.
- Procurement rules.
- Staff training and habit.
- Political blame avoidance.
- Regulatory lock-in.
- Vendor lock-in.
- Coordination costs.
- Stakeholder resistance.
- Professional identity and role protection.

## Reform design principles

- Distinguish institutional memory from institutional paralysis.
- Map switching costs before proposing reform.
- Provide transition support.
- Preserve essential continuity.
- Create staged implementation.
- Reduce uncertainty through pilots.
- Use sunset reviews and periodic default audits.
- Build legitimacy around change.
- Compensate or support those facing concentrated losses.
- Track distributional consequences.

Institutional inertia is behavioral, structural, and political at the same time.
