# Research Design Notes

## Core research question

How do behavioral frictions, defaults, present bias, loss aversion, social norms, price premiums, and income constraints affect household adoption of lower-impact consumption options?

## Economic structure

The scaffold treats adoption as a utility comparison between a conventional option and a sustainable option. Adoption depends on:

- perceived quality or convenience
- price premium
- effort and friction costs
- present bias
- loss aversion relative to the status quo
- perceived environmental benefit
- social-norm signal
- default status
- income and affordability pressure
- quality uncertainty
- policy treatment regime

## Empirical extensions

The synthetic examples can be extended into real empirical designs:

1. Randomized controlled trial of default settings.
2. Difference-in-differences analysis of phased municipal policy rollout.
3. Event-study design around introduction of green defaults or subsidies.
4. Discrete-choice experiment with randomized frames and price premiums.
5. Structural or semi-structural adoption model with heterogeneous behavioral parameters.
6. Welfare analysis incorporating private utility, external benefits, fiscal costs, and distributional incidence.

## Identification cautions

Real policy evaluation must address selection bias, endogenous policy adoption, nonparallel pre-trends, spillovers, anticipation effects, attrition, measurement error, and heterogeneous treatment effects.
