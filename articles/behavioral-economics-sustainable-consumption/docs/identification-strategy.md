# Identification Strategy Notes

This scaffold includes synthetic examples and does not estimate causal effects from real data. For real-world economist-grade research, the following identification strategies may be appropriate.

## Randomized controlled trial

Randomize households or users into information-only, default, subsidy, social-norm, and combined policy arms. Estimate intent-to-treat and treatment-on-treated effects.

## Difference-in-differences

Use staggered rollout of a sustainable-consumption policy across jurisdictions, retailers, utilities, or platforms. Test pre-trends and estimate heterogeneous effects by income group, baseline behavior, and infrastructure access.

## Event study

Estimate dynamic effects before and after policy adoption. Check anticipation effects, persistence, and post-treatment adaptation.

## Discrete choice experiment

Randomize prices, frames, labels, quality guarantees, defaults, and social-norm signals to estimate willingness to pay, loss aversion, and policy response.

## Welfare analysis

Combine adoption estimates with private costs and benefits, environmental externalities, fiscal costs, and distributional weights. Report assumptions transparently.
