# Behavioral Economics and Sustainable Consumption

This article-level folder provides a professional economist-grade computational scaffold for studying sustainable consumption through behavioral economics. It is designed for economists, policy analysts, computational social scientists, sustainability researchers, and decision scientists who want reproducible examples that connect theory, data structure, simulation, causal inference, and welfare analysis.

## Research purpose

The scaffold formalizes sustainable consumption as a decision problem shaped by prices, defaults, present bias, loss aversion, social norms, friction costs, environmental concern, income constraints, and policy design. It includes synthetic microdata and reproducible workflows that demonstrate how to analyze adoption, treatment effects, heterogeneity, distributional consequences, and welfare under competing behavioral-policy regimes.

## Included workflows

- Synthetic household microdata generation
- Behavioral adoption model with defaults, frictions, present bias, loss aversion, social norms, and income constraints
- Discrete-choice style utility comparison
- Policy microsimulation across information-only, default, subsidy, and combined regimes
- Welfare accounting with private welfare, external benefits, fiscal cost, and distributional incidence
- Difference-in-differences and event-study scaffolds for evaluating policy rollout
- Sensitivity analysis across behavioral parameters
- SQL schema for experiment, policy, and microdata provenance
- Stata, R, Python, and Julia economist-oriented workflows
- Systems-language examples for reproducible simulation kernels and command-line scaffolding

## Responsible use

This scaffold uses synthetic data only. It is intended for research design, teaching, reproducible workflows, and transparent policy analysis. It should not be used as a real policy evaluation, consumer scoring system, individual behavioral prediction tool, or operational decision engine without real data governance, identification strategy, peer review, and ethical review.
